LEVEL 3 Where are receptor cells found? - in the sense organs

LEVEL 3 Where do motor neurones carry impulses from and to? - from Central Nervous System (CNS) to Effectors (muscles or glands)

LEVEL 3 List the parts of the nervous system. - Central Nervous System (CNS): brain and spinal cord and Peripheral Nervous System (PNS)

LEVEL 4 Describe the structure of a sensory neurone. - carry electrical impulses from receptor cells to the Central Nervous System (brain and spinal cord); Cell Body: Located on a branch off the middle of the neurone, containing the nucleus and standard cell organelles. Dendron: A single, long nerve fiber that carries impulses towards the cell body from the receptors. Axon: A single, shorter nerve fiber that carries impulses away from the cell body to the CNS. Myelin Sheath: An insulating fatty layer that surrounds the dendron and axon, speeding up nerve impulses. Dendrites: Branching nerve endings at the start (on the dendron) that connect to receptors. Axon Terminals: Branching endings at the far end (on the axon) that pass the signal to the next neurone.

LEVEL 4 How are motor and relay neurones similar? - Motor and relay neurones are similar in that they both act as nerve cells that carry electrical impulses. They also share core structural features, containing a cell body with a nucleus, dendrites to receive signals, and an axon to transmit signals away. Furthermore, both rely on chemicals to transmit impulses across synapses.

LEVEL 5 Describe the function of the reflex arc. - The function of a reflex arc is to provide a rapid, automatic, and involuntary response to a stimulus. Its primary purpose is to protect the body from harm (such as moving your hand away from a hot object) without waiting for conscious thought from the brain.

LEVEL 5 Why are reflexes useful? - Reflexes are automatic, rapid, and involuntary responses to a stimulus. Their primary purpose is protection and survival.

LEVEL 5 How is a sensory neurone well adapted to its function? - Long Axons and Dendrons: They have long fibres so messages can travel quickly across large distances in the body.Myelin Sheath: The fibres are insulated by a fatty myelin sheath. This speeds up the transmission of electrical impulses by forcing them to jump between the gaps.Cell Body in the Middle: Their cell body branches off to the side, allowing uninterrupted and direct signal flow straight from the receptor to the CNS.Dendrites: They have tiny branching extensions that connect directly to sensory receptors to pick up stimuli.

LEVEL 6 What happens at a synapse? - A synapse is a tiny gap between two neurons. Because electrical impulses cannot "jump" this gap, the signal is briefly converted into a chemical message. These chemicals diffuse across the gap and bind to receptors on the next neuron, triggering a new electrical impulse.

LEVEL 6 Why does the reflex arc bring about a faster response? - The reflex arc is faster because the nerve pathway is much shorter and direct. It directs impulses through the spinal cord or an unconscious part of the central nervous system (CNS), bypassing the conscious brain entirely. Because the brain doesn't have to process the information, the body saves vital time and prevents injury.

LEVEL 7 Compare and contrast reflex actions and processed responses. - Reflex actions are rapid, automatic, and involuntary responses to stimuli that bypass the conscious brain to protect the body from harm. In contrast, processed (voluntary) responses are slower, conscious actions that require the brain to evaluate information and decide on an appropriate course of action.
